# English correctness coverage

Correctness runs in every mode: grammar/spelling/punctuation, then an optional tone, then grammar/spelling/punctuation again. In Fix mode, Smart grammar (GECToR, on by default since 0.2) adds a second opinion on top of the rules. Each correctness phase repeats to a bounded stable result, and the final composed edit plan is checked against the actual rewritten text. Original scalar anchors preserve UTF-16 ranges across stages. Corrections remain reviewable; passive suggestions never apply automatically.

Current assets: 255 contextual grammar rules, 154 phrase corrections, a local 132,786-entry lexical export, and 71,372 attributed English frequency entries adapted from wordfreq 3.1.1. Token analysis adds verb morphology, local clause constraints, pronoun/sentence capitalization, accidental function-word repetition, missing sentence boundaries and punctuation spacing. Tone rules are separate and never run in Fix. Rule counts are not accuracy scores.

**What the rules cover and what Smart grammar adds.** The rules are exact and explainable: every one has a reason, a positive and a negative case, and provenance. In 0.2 they became much more precise (run-on sentences, agreement, abbreviations such as p.m. and i.e., commas), and spelling stopped "correcting" Hinglish, Indian English vocabulary, Latin phrases, compounds, words macOS knows and both dialects. The GECToR model then tags words in each new sentence (keep, replace, append, verb form, plural) and catches wider patterns such as a wrong preposition or a verb form that depends on its neighbour. Its edits are accepted only when they pass Parzr's guards and never override a rule edit; the guards are listed in [architecture](architecture.md#smart-grammar-gector). Measured accuracy is in [model evaluation](model-evaluation.md).

The 317-case authored workplace regression corpus includes corrections and valid contexts, plus separate Unicode, source-map, negation, entities, protected structures, composed fixes and every-mode invariants. Passing it proves those cases; it is not a representative English benchmark and does not establish general precision or recall.

The [1,000-paragraph challenge](../benchmarks/README.md) includes spelling, grammar and punctuation combinations plus 100 clean controls, and a 2,000-paragraph set with 200 clean controls repeats the same grammar families on new topics. The 1,000 set is a development challenge: failures in it informed these checker changes, and the 2,000 set checks that the fixes generalize to new vocabulary, not to new structures. Its exact-reference score must not be described as independent held-out accuracy. Fresh regression cases cover the reported failures, valid counterexamples and linked minimal edits that preserve a formatted proper name during inversion. Native TextEdit QA separately checks typing-driven marks, compact inline acceptance, formatting, caret restoration and Undo.

“All English grammar” is the coverage ambition, not a verified claim. Many valid constructions are ambiguous without wider discourse. We must expand detection and explanations without guessing changes to names, facts, negation, timing or intended tone. Uncertain spelling candidates are labeled lower-confidence and remain explicit review choices.

## Rule packs

`engine/rules/packs/*.json` hold one category of contextual rules each (see the README there). The verb complementation pack (`verb-complements.json`, ids `vcomp.`) covers gerund versus infinitive after a governing verb, a missing or surplus "to", "to" followed by the wrong verb form, a person plus a bare verb, "suggest me to go", adjective plus "to" plus -ing, and a transitive verb left without its object; its coverage map and the constructions it leaves out on purpose are in [rule-packs/verb-complements.md](rule-packs/verb-complements.md). Its rules are generated and self-checked by `scripts/gen-verb-complements.py`.

The punctuation pack (`punctuation-rules.json`, ids `punct.`) adds only what a regex can add beside the Rust punctuation checks: hyphens in compound modifiers, number-and-unit modifiers, ordinals and prefixes (`data driven approach`, `a three story house`, `twenty first`, `self esteem`, `mother in law`), missing or stray apostrophes in fixed frames (`last years budget`, `two weeks notice`, `Mothers Day`, `its'`, `he work's`, `do n't`), spacing glued or doubled around marks, ellipsis and brackets, `e.g.`/`i.e.`/`et al.`, a colon after `including` before a name, commas after sentence openers and after `said`, and wh-questions ended with a period. Its coverage map (what is left to the engine or left out on purpose) is in [rule-packs/punctuation-rules.md](rule-packs/punctuation-rules.md); the rules are generated and self-checked by `scripts/punctuation-pack/build.py`.
The clauses and conjunctions pack (`clauses-conjunctions.json`, ids `clause.`, 185 rules) covers doubled linking words ("Although it rained but we went", "Even though … but", "Despite … but", "Because … so", "Due to … so", "Because … therefore / that's why"), a preposition where a clause follows ("because of he was late", "due to she left", "despite it rained", "during I was away", "in case of you need", "Although the rain, we went"), paired conjunctions ("neither … or", "either … nor", "between … or", "no sooner … when", "hardly … than", "not only …, and also", "the more …, more …"), the if / whether / in case family ("if or not", "whether if", "unless if", "if in case", "in the event if", "even it rains"), "who" for a thing ("the file who contains"), a pronoun repeating the relative word ("the song that I wrote it"), "the reason … is because", "for to", "how come that", "so therefore", "but however", "Although, I think", "because that" and split connectors (where ever, further more). Its coverage map, the constructions left out on purpose and the problems found in base passes are in [rule-packs/clauses-conjunctions.md](rule-packs/clauses-conjunctions.md). Its rules are generated and self-checked by `scripts/gen-clauses-conjunctions.py`.
The nouns and plurals pack (`nouns-plurals.json`, ids `noun.`, generated by `scripts/nouns-plurals-pack/build.py`; coverage map in [rule-packs/nouns-plurals.md](rule-packs/nouns-plurals.md)) covers noun number and countability beyond the engine's own `grammar.noun_number` and the articles pack: mass nouns given a plural (knowledges, luggages, feedbacks, datas, newses, livestocks; researches, evidences, trainings, stuffs, staffs, peoples, medias in noun contexts only), wrong plurals of irregular and Latin/Greek nouns (tooths, mices, sheeps, criterias, phenomenas, analysises, wolfs, knifes, wifes, lifes, halfs, mother-in-laws), -s for -es and -ys for -ies slips the spelling pass leaves unfixed or fixes wrongly, childrens/womens before a verb or with an apostrophe after the s, a singular unit after a number (5 year ago, for 3 month, is 5 year old, 10 kilometer away, 20 dollar, for 4 person), a singular noun after a lot of / a couple of / a number of / these / the two / hundreds of / three more / most of the / 40 percent of the, two hundreds, one years, per hours, a good friends, and apostrophe plurals after "lots of" and for acronyms. Plurals that are also possessives or verbs (the wolfs den, he wolfs down) are flagged only where a plural verb or the clause end proves the plural. Names, decades, hyphenated number modifiers and what other packs own are left alone.
The capitalization pack (`capitalization-rules.json`, ids `caps.`, about 1,900 rules generated by `scripts/rule-pack-gen/capitalization/`; coverage map in [rule-packs/capitalization-rules.md](rule-packs/capitalization-rules.md)) adds what the gazetteer misses: lowercase countries, cities, regions, languages, nationalities, holidays, deities and scriptures; names that are also words (`turkey`, `china`, `polish`, `dutch`, `lent`) only in a frame that cannot be the common word; brands and products (`ebay`, `xbox`, `google maps`, `microsoft teams`, and `zoom`, `slack`, `python`, `windows` only with their product frame); about 140 acronyms (`api`, `url`, `json`, `ceo`, `nasa`, `fbi`...) with plurals; titles directly before a capitalized name (`with president Obama`); abbreviated months and days with a date, `bc`/`ad`, time zones; stray capitals a compass point after a travel verb, a school subject after a study verb (Style), `, But` after a lowercase word, a lowercase quotation opening after `said,`. Tokens that mix letters and digits, file names and name-tagged words are protected by the engine, so they are not covered.

## Extending coverage

Rules live in `engine/rules/grammar.json`, `usage.json` (real-word confusions and word-choice usage) and `phrases.json`. Every contextual rule has a stable ID, target capture, exact replacement, explanation, confidence, provenance, positive case and valid negative case. A rule with a `moved` capture after its target removes the target and inserts the replacement after the capture as one linked pair, so a name is moved but never respelled ("Me and Sarah are" to "Sarah and I are"). Add end-to-end corpus examples with expected output and intent checks; include counterexamples beyond the regex fixture itself. Run all grammar pipeline, Unicode, rich-text and editor tests. Do not silently drop invalid packs or regexes.

Licensed adaptations retain attribution and source/version hashes. Grammar rules execute in Parzr's own VM. The independent wordfreq data asset and its credits ship inside the app and DMG; its Python exporter is a build-time tool. Broad upstream benchmark numbers are not Parzr's numbers.

Next coverage milestones: an independent held-out workplace/general prose corpus (BEA-2019, CoNLL-2014 and JFLEG now measure general learner and student English, see [model evaluation](model-evaluation.md)); grammatical dependency/phrase analysis; more contextual confusion rules; phonetic article handling; structured punctuation findings; candidate confidence calibration; safe statistical ranking and tagging, never free generative replacement in the automatic path. Measure false positives and missed errors by family and dialect before promoting a rule to automatic high-confidence status.
